Entry for the [make.fun bounty "The Worst App Idea Ever Pitched"](https://make.fun/bounty/u-plucky-sparrow-67-the-worst-app-idea-ever-pitched-a0396b23): one sentence, at most 240 characters, pitching the worst and most useless app imaginable — a product that sounds real, a founder who sounds certain, and a function nobody on earth needs.

## The pitch

> Meet Blinkr, the $10-a-month app that counts every blink you make, syncs your blinks to the cloud, and sends a weekly leaderboard ranking your blinking against your friends, because your eyelids deserve analytics.

(213 characters, one sentence.)

## Why this wins

- **Specific:** a named product, a real price ($10 a month), and three concrete features — blink counting, cloud sync, and a weekly leaderboard. It reads like a real App Store listing, not a vague joke.
- **Funny:** the escalation is the joke. Counting blinks is silly; syncing them to the cloud is sillier; ranking your friends' blinking is absurd — and the deadpan punchline, "because your eyelids deserve analytics," is delivered with total founder confidence.
- **Perfectly pointless:** blinking is involuntary, unfixable, and unrankable. No human being has ever needed, or will ever need, analytics for their eyelids — and the app charges a subscription for it anyway.

## Run

```sh
node --experimental-strip-types main.ts
```

`main.ts` scores six candidate pitches on the judge's three axes — specificity, funniness, pointlessness (pointlessness weighted double, since "perfectly pointless" is the deciding criterion) — and writes the winning single sentence to `/output/entry.txt`.
